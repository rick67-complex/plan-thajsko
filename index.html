    Before you go — six things with deadlines
    • Sun 26 Jul — the TDAC window opens. All six in one session at tdac.immigration.go.th. It can't be filed earlier, and without it you're sent to the fallback queue at Bangkok.
    • 29–30 Jul are dry days. No alcohol sold anywhere for 48 hours — shops, 7-Elevens, standalone bars. Buy duty-free in transit: 1 L each, 6 L for the group.
    • The minivan can't climb your hill. Agree a ring-road pickup point when you book the Koh Tao boat — not at 06:00 in the dark.
    • Koh Nang Yuan: cash only, no plastic bottles, no drones. Day visitors are gated 10:00–15:00 — a tour boat gets you in ahead of it.
    • Walking Street is Friday only, 17:00–23:00. That one fact is why Fri 7 Aug sits where it does.
    • Sunset is 18:40 all trip. Scooters need an IDP with category A or your insurance is void.
    Where the weather numbers come from

    Days 1–3 — real forecast. Live model data pulled 24 July. The horizon is 8 days, which reaches 31 July and no further.

    Days 4–8 — 10-day outlook. Inside the window where models retain partial skill, roughly 60–70% on general conditions in this season. Shown as ranges because a single number here would be false precision.

    Days 9–11 — August averages. Past useful forecast skill, so these are climate normals (climate-data.org 1999–2019, weather-and-climate.com 1990–2020). Sites advertising “30-day forecasts” are publishing climatology with a forecast label — several say so in their own small print.

    The useful finding: 1–10 August is historically the driest stretch of the month on Samui — about 7.8–10.7 mm against 15.9 mm in the last third. Your days 4–11 all sit inside it. Samui also runs drier in August than most of Thailand because of its own monsoon timing: roughly 103–130 mm over 12–17 rain days, against 296 mm in October. Rain here is short afternoon showers, not washouts.

    Rain % for days 9–11 is derived, not observed: 12–17 rain days ÷ 31 days = 39–55%, rounded to 40–55.

    Re-check on 30 July (brings 2–6 Aug into real forecast) and 2 August (brings the Koh Tao day into range).

    About the Google Maps setup in this file

    The map is the Maps JavaScript API with a custom dark style, so the photo markers and styling are real rather than a filter. Three things worth knowing:

    1. Enable one API. Cloud console → APIs & Services → Library → Maps JavaScript API → Enable. That’s the only one this file calls.

    2. Restrict the key to it. Under Credentials → your key → API restrictions, allow Maps JavaScript API only. Dynamic Maps is $7 per 1,000 loads with 10,000 free every month — eleven day-tabs across six people won’t come close, so expect ฿0. Restricting the key is what stops anyone lifting it and spending it on paid APIs you aren’t using.

    3. The photos cost nothing. They were fetched from Google Places once and written into this file, so there are no Places API calls at runtime. Place Details with photos bills at the Enterprise tier — about $20 per 1,000 with only 1,000 free per month — which is why fetching them live on every page view would have been the expensive way to do this. Photos are by Google Maps contributors; each stop name links to its listing where the credits live.
